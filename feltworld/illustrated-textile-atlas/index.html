Feltworld rule: hair must always be hair

Accepted direction from Axel. For every human-like character—including gnomes and fairies—hair is special. It must have a distinct treatment and remain recognisable as hair, separate from the character’s felt body surface.

Preserve strands or locks, roots and parting, directional growth, length, movement and intentional styling: a fringe, braid, curl or swept hairstyle should retain its own structure. Hair can be made with tactile fibres within the handmade world, but must not become generic body fuzz, a solid felt cap or an undifferentiated wool mound.

Application to the studies: the BTS animal’s body pile, trimming and brushing are useful fur references. They do not define how a gnome’s or fairy’s hair should look. When developing a human-like character, specify the hairstyle and hair behaviour separately from body felting and costume construction. Retain Nomie’s established two braids/fringe and Jack’s swept hair; wet hair still reads as hair.

Material distinction: this is a visual and behavioural requirement, not a requirement to use literal human hair. Animal fur and Lumi’s fine ethereal pile retain their separate directions. This accepted rule adds no new hairstyle or identity and does not replace existing artwork.

Beautiful, highly organised fluff

The phrase captures the surface beautifully. What organises it is local consolidation, shaped volume, direction, layered colour and intentional edges. Needle-felting is itself a construction method. It can coexist with rigid cores, fitted panels, separate attachments and true weaving.

A felting needle’s small barbs/notches engage fibres and repeated penetration entangles and compacts them. That general mechanism is supported by FibreCraft’s needle guide. The source video directly shows repeated needle work and resulting forms; it does not expose that microscopic mechanism or identify the needle gauge. This is dry needle work in the observed passages; no wet-felting step is documented here.

  1. Place volume: a tuft, thin layer or prepared piece is held against a support or existing shape. The stair core is visible; the character’s initial core is not.
  2. Consolidate locally: the needle targets particular planes, rims, seams, grooves, ear interiors and fingers. We see distinct shapes, not equal fuzz everywhere.
  3. Add colour as fibre: blue stripes, plate decoration, checks, fruit marks and little pictures are worked into fibrous surfaces. A represented weave/print can be made without actual woven cloth.
  4. Join and fit: shelves, trims, drawers and garment panels are placed, shaped and refined. Some hidden connections remain uncertain.
  5. Groom selected regions: scissors and brush control the longer head fur. The torso and ears retain a clearer, more compact silhouette.

Useful correction to the Tulip study: hardness of appearance is not proof of hard substrate. This film demonstrates wool stove, plate and wood-like furniture surfaces. Tulip’s glossy crockery remains an appearance-based ceramic inference; it is not reclassified solely because this different production uses felt.

What this changes for new character development

We can now brief the making logic before requesting a look. The demonstrated character segment begins around 10:32 with an already formed body: it covers refinement, grooming and dressing, not every step of an original build.

1. Silhouette and density board

One original proposed character, same pose and colour, three surface treatments: compact shaped volume; compact volume with fine continuous surface pile; selected longer fur at one identity-bearing region.

Review criterion: Body and palm stay readable at page size; softness appears through fine fibres without erasing anatomy.

2. Hand, ear and muzzle studies

Use a neutral character fragment. Compare a shallow palm with clean digit divisions, a thin rimmed ear and softly bounded muzzle/cheek colour.

Review criterion: Each small volume has a purpose, thickness and negative space. Avoid random puncture marks as a universal texture.

3. Directional colour and pile map

Mark where colour layers overlap and where pile runs, gathers or becomes longer. Use one face/body view plus one rear view.

Review criterion: Dominant colour remains stable around the volume; longer fibres are local and do not turn the whole body into shag.

4. Needle-felted garment versus woven garment

Keep the same proposed character and garment shape. Compare fine fibre-worked stripes on a thin felt panel with a true woven panel, showing a sleeve join and hem.

Review criterion: Pattern, thickness, fold behaviour and body-to-cloth contrast are explicit. Do not imply removal, knitting or embroidery without evidence.

5. Character and useful object scale board

Place the full proposed character beside its own cup, pan/tool handle and table/seat. Include a separate workshop reference panel with a maker’s finger/tool.

Review criterion: The character can plausibly grip and reach the objects. Workshop tools remain clearly identified as making references.

6. Pose-support and grooming plan

For a future physical puppet, outline support/joint questions separately from visible pile. For an illustration, specify stable volume and regional trimming/brushing language.

Review criterion: Hidden armature choices remain open; no unsupported claim of a particular skeleton. Lumi and all approved characters retain their established identities.

New-character prompt vocabulary: Original proposed [character identity and silhouette], made as needle-felted wool sculpture with coherent shaped volume; [region-specific fibre density/pile length and direction]; softly integrated [colour transitions]; shaped palm/ear/muzzle volumes; separately fitted [garment substrate, pattern and thickness]; small irregularities that follow the making process; consistent scale beside [one useful prop]; lighting that reveals fibres without hiding form. Keep design references and approval status explicit.

Housing: shelter, threshold and toy-scale wonder

Axel identifies an important surface reading: the homes look like porcelain, moulded plastic or children’s modelling clay. Rounded walls, thick cap-like roofs, softened edges and smooth local highlights create a small, holdable toy-home appearance. Those are visible qualities, not a confirmed recipe or physical material identification. Gloss alone cannot distinguish ceramic glaze from polished polymer, painted clay or lighting. These homes matter because they are inhabitable miniature refuges: doors at ground level, steps, windows, curtains, neighbours, routes and warm light. Oversized roofs and huge surrounding plants make the inhabitants feel small while the interiors feel safe. A teacup home in Feltworld remains our own direction; the clip does not supply proof of one.

What makes the world touchable

  1. Construction comes before fuzz. A leaf folds, a stem wraps, a sleeve knits, a skirt drapes, a wing transmits light. The material changes the object’s shape and behaviour.
  2. There are several scales of texture. Large forms remain readable, medium seams and folds show how things were made, and fine fibres appear chiefly at edges and lit surfaces.
  3. Adjacent materials disagree usefully. Glossy eyes sit in matte wool. Fine dress cloth hangs over compact limbs. A thick knitted blanket meets a hard stone-like floor.
  4. Light reveals the material. Side lighting catches fine hairs; backlighting reveals a woven grid; tiny highlights distinguish crockery and wire. The night scenes preserve texture without uniform glow.
  5. Ordinary craft objects provide scale. Pins, thread, spools and patchwork tell us how small Tulip is. They serve actions rather than merely decorate the frame.
  6. Imperfections have structure. Uneven stitches, cut edges, flyaways and irregular painted marks follow real construction. Random grain everywhere would not recreate the effect.

Important correction to the broad first impression: the detailed wing close-ups reveal an actual grid and sheen, so “paper-like” is insufficient for those wings. Some plant and butterfly sheets remain ambiguous. Likewise, the film does not establish crochet for every moss patch or lily pad.

Five proposed trials worth proposing

These are written briefs only. No ImageGen calls or new artwork were made. If selected, use one existing scene or a neutral swatch first; keep original concepts and current character identity references together.

1. A leaf and flower construction board

Compare one cupped petal, one folded blade and one wrapped stem at the same scale. Include a few fine edge hairs, darker overlap shadows and a controlled violet centre.

Review criterion: A readable botanical silhouette at page size, with visible thickness and construction in the close-up. Avoid rounded broccoli-like clusters; retain our accepted elongated canopy language.

2. Sanctuary blanket and garment contrast

Keep the approved characters and interior. Compare compact wool limbs against the established crochet blanket, one fine woven cushion and one restrained sewn edge. No new outfit is implied.

Review criterion: The fibre density and cloth technique must differ. At thumbnail size the body should remain clear; at native size individual crochet structure, seams and folds should be visible.

3. Textile water or snow swatch

Compare a dark lake base with sparse long directional fibres against dense undirected wool. Separately compare compact textile snow with a few loose edge strands.

Review criterion: The water reads as flowing and partially transparent; the snow reads as resting mass. They should not be interchangeable blue/white fluffy overlays.

4. Tree bark and one warm window

Use our approved tree-home geometry. Compare broad longitudinal bark relief with fine sparse surface fibres and a single backlit woven window.

Review criterion: The architecture stays the same; bark is structured rather than uniformly fuzzy, and the woven diffuser remains visible without excessive glow.

5. One restrained luminous accent

Compare matte wool beside a thin woven/gauze swatch with a little transmitted light and angle-dependent sheen. Keep it as a material board or apply only to an existing approved aurora/eye treatment later.

Review criterion: The accent remains physically legible and subordinate. No new wings, magic power, repeated lanterns, plastic skin or redesign of approved irises.

A reusable construction prompt

Describe each object as a made thing: [material and fibre density], [construction technique], [edge and thickness], [overlap/contact], [light response], [scale relative to the character]. Then specify the story action, established identity references and quiet composition. Add fine fibres after the form is clear.

Keep our canon: fibrous faces and hands; approved eye/nose exceptions; glazed pottery homes; restrained backgrounds; one clear story moment; no excessive glitter or repeated lanterns.
